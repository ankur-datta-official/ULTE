Snapshot loader strategy: PostgresRecoveryBootLoader requires the committed
PostgresSnapshotExecutor. Each boot calls snapshot() exactly once. All D2C
capability/catalog checks and recovery, lease, checkpoint, pending, broker,
outcome, creation/adoption, and terminal receipt reads use its one transaction.
The new recovery, keyed pending (including RESOLVED), terminal-resolved pending
enumeration, and lease loaders are transaction-bound SELECTs without row locks.
Existing mappers and receipt loaders are reused. No nested transaction,
executor.query(), broker/provider call, or write occurs in boot.

Classifier: terminal evidence is evaluated before lease and READY precedence.
A V2 recovery row yields TERMINAL_NON_SUBMISSION only for the complete exact
receipt, broker, resolved pending, creation proof, checkpoint ancestry, frozen
identity/mode/instrument/risk/latest-R, revision/fence, and noncontradictory
outcome tuple. Later lease-fence advancement is allowed while the historical
terminal commit fence remains tied to the receipt and pending resolution.
The result is stopped terminal authority with no executable runner state.

Bare historical FAILED_NOT_SUBMITTED plus PENDING and no closure is rejected
with TERMINAL_NON_SUBMISSION_PROOF_UNAVAILABLE. No proof or reviewed legacy
attestation is invented. Database boot never emits
TERMINAL_NON_SUBMISSION_REQUIRED. Orphan terminal-resolved pending evidence,
partial/missing/mismatched receipt, wrong broker facts, malformed V2 recovery,
invalid proof or ancestry, wrong resolution/ref/revision/fence, and linked
contradictory outcomes fail closed. Terminal and partial terminal states do not
fall through to READY. The original nonterminal C1 classification remains.
There is no production RecoveryBootResult dispatcher to modify; no dispatcher
or C2B3 cutover was created.
